## Per-model reports

### RVC (Retrieval-based Voice Conversion WebUI) (voice-conversion)
- license: MIT (RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI). Freely redistributable. Note: pretrained base models (HuBERT/ContentVec, RMVPE) carry their own upstream licenses but are permissively usable locally; no gated weights. | free-redistributable: True
- fidelity: 8/10 — timbre match is excellent with 30-60 min clean data; top-1 retrieval (feature index) suppresses tone leakage. Ceiling is timbre only: prosody/cadence come from the DONOR performance you feed in, not from the model.
- data needed: 10 min minimum works; 30-60 min clean mono 44.1kHz gives near-indistinguishable timbre. More data mainly helps the retrieval index, not the decoder.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: yes** — Training runs on Apple Silicon via PyTorch MPS but is slow and historically CPU-heavy. Issue #767 reports an M1 MacBook Pro taking 6+ hours for a 30-min dataset with the user unsure the GPU was even engaged (https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI/issues/767). MPS support was an open 'help wanted' enhancement as of issue #136 (https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI/issues/136) and issue #575 (https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI/issues/575). No CUDA-only dependencies (no DeepSpeed/bitsandbytes/flash-attn/xformers in the training loop) — it is plain PyTorch + numpy, which is why it degrades to MPS/CPU rather than failing. Community macOS forks exist (NevilPatel01/RVC-WebUI-MacOS, qingbo1011/RVC-WebUI-MacOS) and a pure-MLX inference port (Acelogic/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-MLX, ~8.71x faster inference than MPS) — but the MLX port accelerates INFERENCE, not training.
- inference AS: mps — works on MPS with CPU fallback; MLX port gives ~8.7x faster inference. Real-time capable on M-series.
- prosody/style: No native text-driven prosody. As a converter, cadence/emphasis/emotion are inherited from the source donor audio. Manual controls: pitch transpose (semitones), index/retrieval rate (0-1, timbre vs artifact tradeoff), median filter radius (breathiness), RMS volume-envelope mix, and F0 method (RMVPE/Crepe/FCPE). No pause/rate/style tokens.
- latency: Inference is fast/near-real-time on M-series (MPS or MLX). Training is the bottleneck: hours per model on Apple Silicon.
- verdict: Serious contender for local + free + Mac-trainable and delivers excellent timbre cloning — but it is a voice CONVERTER, not TTS: 'indistinguishable' only if you feed it a real donor performance, and Apple-Silicon training is slow.
- urls: https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI, https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI/issues/767, https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI/issues/136, https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI/issues/575, https://github.com/Acelogic/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-MLX, https://github.com/NevilPatel01/RVC-WebUI-MacOS

### so-vits-svc (SoftVC VITS Singing Voice Conversion) (voice-conversion)
- license: MIXED — original svc-develop-team/so-vits-svc is AGPL-3.0 AND archived read-only since Nov 2023, with non-commercial/academic and consent clauses ('academic purposes', fictional characters, must credit source vocals). The actively maintained fork voicepaw/so-vits-svc-fork is MIT-licensed and the practical choice. | free-redistributable: True
- fidelity: 8/10 (fork, well-trained) — strong timbre/naturalness on both speech and singing; arguably better pitch handling than RVC for sung/expressive material. Same ceiling caveat: prosody comes from the source clip.
- data needed: ~30-60 min clean audio for near-indistinguishable results; clips sliced to 5-15s, 44.1kHz mono. Diffusion head (fork) improves detail with more data.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: yes** — Training confirmed working on Apple Silicon but very slow. Discussion #851 (M1 Pro): training proceeds only with PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK=1 because aten::_weight_norm_interface had no MPS kernel; ~15 min/epoch, projecting 2-3 weeks for 2000 epochs at 60-80% GPU util (https://github.com/voicepaw/so-vits-svc-fork/discussions/851). Issue #170 documents the MPS aten::_weight_norm NotImplementedError and the CPU-fallback workaround (https://github.com/svc-develop-team/so-vits-svc/issues/170). A PyTorch fix for the blocking op was merged into nightlies, reducing fallback need. Austin Yip trained a full voice clone on a Mac Studio (https://austinyip.medium.com/i-cloned-my-own-voice-to-create-an-ai-austin-yip-using-my-mac-studio-957e159de28d). No CUDA-only libs (no DeepSpeed/bitsandbytes/flash-attn) in the loop — plain PyTorch, so MPS/CPU works. Apple-Silicon fork: audiohacking/so-vits-svc-fork-osx.
- inference AS: mps — works on MPS with CPU fallback; realtime fork exists. Some report inference issues on M1 requiring CPU (issue #687).
- prosody/style: No text-driven prosody — converter, so cadence/emotion come from the source clip. Controls: pitch/transpose, F0 predictor (RMVPE/FCPE/Crepe/Dio), optional shallow-diffusion depth for timbre detail, cluster/feature-retrieval ratio for timbre-vs-clarity, and NSF-HiFiGAN vocoder. No pause/rate/emphasis tokens.
- latency: Inference near-real-time on M-series (fork has realtime mode). Training is the severe bottleneck on Apple Silicon: days-to-weeks for full convergence due to MPS fallback.
- verdict: Viable local + free (via the MIT voicepaw fork) + Mac-trainable, with cloning quality on par with or better than RVC for expressive/sung audio — but Apple-Silicon training is the slowest here (MPS fallback, weeks), and like RVC it is a converter, not TTS.
- urls: https://github.com/voicepaw/so-vits-svc-fork, https://github.com/voicepaw/so-vits-svc-fork/discussions/851, https://github.com/svc-develop-team/so-vits-svc, https://github.com/svc-develop-team/so-vits-svc/issues/170, https://github.com/voicepaw/so-vits-svc-fork/issues/687, https://github.com/audiohacking/so-vits-svc-fork-osx

### XTTS-v2 (Coqui TTS) (TTS)
- license: Code MPL-2.0 (permissive); model weights Coqui Public Model License (CPML) 1.0 — NON-COMMERCIAL only. Coqui Inc. shut down Jan 2024, so no commercial license is purchasable. Fine for personal/local/non-commercial use; NOT freely redistributable for commercial products. | free-redistributable: False
- fidelity: 8/10 — with fine-tuning on 30-60 min it is one of the strongest open cloners; excellent timbre match, but the GPT-2 autoregressive decoder occasionally drifts in pronunciation/prosody near the end of long paragraphs, capping true indistinguishability.
- data needed: 30-60 min clean, single-speaker, 22kHz+; more helps consistency. Zero-shot works from ~6s but fine-tuning on 30-60 min is what pushes toward indistinguishable.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: yes** — Fine-tuning itself is first-class (official Gradio pipeline `xtts_demo.py` trains the GPT layer). Apple-Silicon reality is the catch: MPS is BROKEN for XTTS — coqui-ai/TTS issue #3649 is labelled bug+wontfix, model 'just hangs until Ctrl-C' on MPS (M1 Max), and the documented workaround is device=cpu which 'works just fine and rather quickly' for inference. A dedicated repo (DrewThomasson/finetuneXtts_apple_silicone) explicitly fine-tunes XTTS on an M1 Pro 16GB Mac — but its gpt_train.py contains no MPS device selection, so training falls back to CPU. Net: fine-tuning DOES run end-to-end on Apple Silicon, but on CPU (slow, hours-to-days), not GPU. No CUDA-only ops (DeepSpeed/bitsandbytes) are required for the base training path.
- inference AS: cpu
- prosody/style: Reference-audio conditioning drives timbre + speaking style/emotion (no explicit emotion tags). Native decoding controls: temperature, length_penalty, repetition_penalty, top_k, top_p, and a speed factor. No fine-grained per-word pause/emphasis markup; prosody is implicit from the reference clip and punctuation.
- latency: CPU-only on Mac (MPS hangs); inference is usable but not real-time on longer text. Training on CPU is slow — plan for many hours for a good fine-tune.
- verdict: Serious contender: it is the one of the two that provably fine-tunes AND infers on Apple Silicon (via CPU, MPS broken) and hits high similarity — the trade-off is non-commercial weights and slow CPU-only speed.
- urls: https://github.com/coqui-ai/TTS/issues/3649, https://github.com/DrewThomasson/finetuneXtts_apple_silicone, https://docs.coqui.ai/en/latest/models/xtts.html, https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2/blob/main/LICENSE.txt, https://github.com/coqui-ai/TTS/discussions/4304

### F5-TTS (TTS)
- license: Code MIT (permissive); official pre-trained weights CC-BY-NC-4.0 (non-commercial, due to Emilia in-the-wild training data). An Apache-2.0 re-trained base exists (mrfakename/OpenF5-TTS-Base) for those needing commercial/redistributable weights. | free-redistributable: False
- fidelity: 9/10 potential — flow-matching (DiT) gives best-in-class English naturalness and prosody on long inputs (reported zero-shot speaker similarity ~0.864, silences/cadence sound right); fine-tuning on 30-60 min raises similarity further. Fidelity ceiling is the highest of the two — IF you can train it.
- data needed: 10-60 min clean per speaker; community reports significant quality gains after fine-tuning, but only after long GPU training runs (~hundreds of epochs).
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: no** — This is where it fails the decisive gate. The training code is CUDA-focused: issue #150 ('Finetune on MPS support') documents that the codebase only checks `torch.cuda.is_available()` and lacks Apple-Silicon device logic; no maintainer or user confirms MPS fine-tuning actually works. MPS also can't do float64 ('Cannot convert a MPS Tensor to float64 … MPS doesn't support float64'), which the flow-matching training path can hit. Issues #875/#56 are open feature requests for MPS/CoreML/MLX, unanswered by maintainers. Community fine-tune guidance (discussion #769) assumes an NVIDIA GPU — ~320 epochs / ~20-25k steps ≈ 1 day on a 12GB RTX 4080 with bf16; CPU-only on a Mac would be impractically slow (days-plus). MLX ports (f5-tts-mlx, f5-tts-swift) exist but are inference-oriented, not a proven fine-tune path. Conclusion: no credible evidence of a working local fine-tune on Apple Silicon MPS or CPU.
- inference AS: mps
- prosody/style: Flow-matching, no duration model/phoneme alignment — prosody is implicit from the reference clip and punctuation. Native knobs: NFE steps, sway-sampling coefficient, speed, cross-fade duration, and reference-audio style transfer. No explicit emotion or per-word emphasis tags.
- latency: Inference runs on MPS (pip `f5-tts` selects mps) and there are MLX/CoreML community ports for fast Apple-Silicon inference. Fine-tuning is the problem, not inference.
- verdict: Highest fidelity ceiling of the two, but it FAILS the non-negotiable gate: no working local fine-tune on Apple Silicon (CUDA-focused trainer, MPS float64 limits, unanswered MPS requests) — inference-only on Mac, so disqualified for the train-on-this-Mac requirement.
- urls: https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/issues/150, https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/issues/875, https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/issues/56, https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/discussions/769, https://huggingface.co/mrfakename/OpenF5-TTS-Base, https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS

### Fish-Speech / OpenAudio S1 (S1-mini open weights) (TTS)
- license: Code Apache-2.0; open weights = OpenAudio S1-mini (0.5B) under CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 (non-commercial, share-alike). Full S1 is closed/API-only. Not freely redistributable for commercial use. | free-redistributable: False
- fidelity: 8/10 — trained on ~2M hrs, very strong zero-shot similarity/naturalness and a rich prosody markup; ceiling is high in principle, but real-world fine-tune reliability is shaky (documented gibberish 'awawawa' failures) which caps what you can dependably reach.
- data needed: ~10-30 min of clean audio for LoRA timbre fine-tune; 30-60 min helps naturalness. Docs warn fine-tuning easily overfits timbre.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: no** — Official LoRA fine-tune (python fish_speech/train.py --config-name text2semantic_finetune) is documented but designed for CUDA GPUs; original AR training used 8xH100 for a week (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01156). MPS support in the repo is INFERENCE-ONLY (PR #461 'Support inference on mps device natively', https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/pull/461); macOS native deploy still 'coming soon'. No evidence of anyone fine-tuning on MPS/CPU. Fine-tune reliability itself is questioned: issue #1136 shows a correctly-formatted 211-clip run on an RTX 4090 producing pure gibberish, closed as 'not planned' (https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/issues/1136). S1-mini fine-tune support was itself ambiguous (train.py present in v1.5.0, questioned in #1086, https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/issues/1086). The training stack (PyTorch-Lightning, bf16, flash-attention-style attention) is CUDA-oriented; bitsandbytes/flash-attn have no macOS path.
- inference AS: mps — native MPS inference merged (PR #461), but slow/impractical: community reports ~25-70s per short sentence on Apple Silicon; usable for offline batch, painful for interactive use.
- prosody/style: Strong: inline emotion/tone markers ((angry)(sad)(excited)(whispering) etc.), special event markers (laughing, breathing, sighing), tone/emphasis control, and reference-audio timbre. Emotion is text-driven at synthesis time.
- latency: Apple Silicon inference ~25-70s/sentence (not real-time). Fast on CUDA; MPS is the bottleneck.
- verdict: Not a viable contender for THIS spec: weights are non-commercial (NC) and there is zero evidence fine-tuning works on Apple Silicon — official fine-tune is CUDA-only and even on NVIDIA it has documented gibberish failures. MPS is inference-only and slow.
- urls: https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech, https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/blob/main/docs/en/finetune.md, https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/issues/1136, https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/pull/461, https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/openaudio-s1-mini, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.01156

### IndexTTS-2 (TTS)
- license: Code Apache-2.0, but model WEIGHTS ship under a custom 'bilibili Model Use License Agreement' (LICENSE file), not Apache/MIT. Restricts >100M MAU / >RMB1B revenue orgs (must request separate license), forbids using it to improve commercial AI models, bans high-risk uses, PRC governing law. Effectively gated/restricted, not cleanly free-redistributable. | free-redistributable: False
- fidelity: 9/10 — arXiv 2506.21619 reports SOTA speaker similarity + emotion fidelity with best-in-class emotion/timbre disentanglement and duration control; the strongest achievable-similarity ceiling of the two IF you could fine-tune it locally.
- data needed: ~30-60 min, but note the trainer needs prompt/target PAIRS constructed so the model learns timbre while separating emotion — more data-prep effort than a plain LoRA.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: no** — The OFFICIAL index-tts/index-tts repo is zero-shot inference only — no training code (confirmed in README + issue #501 'Unofficial Training/Finetuning Code for v2', https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts/issues/501). Fine-tuning exists only via community forks: JarodMica/index-tts training_v2 branch (https://github.com/JarodMica/index-tts/tree/training_v2) and MLNekit/index-tts-2-train — both NVIDIA/CUDA-oriented (train_gpt_v2.py, optional DeepSpeed), no Mac/MPS support documented. Official inference itself requires CUDA Toolkit 12.8+ with optional flash-attention/DeepSpeed. Apple Silicon exists ONLY for inference via third-party MLX ports (e.g. vanch007/mlx-indextts2-standard-fp16, a project explicitly to avoid PyTorch MPS memory crashes on M3 Max) and ComfyUI-Index-TTS's MPS mode — none of which train. No evidence anyone has fine-tuned IndexTTS-2 on MPS/CPU.
- inference AS: mps — via community MLX port (inference-only) and ComfyUI MPS support; official PyTorch path is CUDA-only and reportedly crashes/OOMs on MPS without the MLX rewrite.
- prosody/style: Best-in-class: explicit emotion-timbre disentanglement (independent control of who vs how), 8-dim emotion vector, text-description-to-emotion, emotion-from-audio-prompt, and explicit token-count DURATION control. Pinyin pronunciation control for Chinese. The richest native prosody/style surface of the two.
- latency: Autoregressive; fine on CUDA. On Apple Silicon only the MLX inference port is practical; PyTorch MPS path is unstable/memory-crashing per community projects.
- verdict: Highest quality ceiling and best prosody controls, but disqualified for THIS spec: fine-tuning exists only as unofficial CUDA trainers, Apple Silicon is inference-only (MLX), no evidence of local MPS/CPU training, and weights carry a restrictive non-free license.

### GPT-SoVITS (v2Pro / v4) (hybrid)
- license: MIT (code + weights). Free, open-source, redistributable for local and commercial use. No gated weights, no non-commercial clause. Only soft ask is ethical-use disclosure. | free-redistributable: True
- fidelity: 9/10 — best-in-class few-shot speaker similarity; community/blog tests report 80-95% similarity from seconds of audio, and a 30-60 min fine-tune pushes timbre + prosody realism close to indistinguishable. v4/v2Pro produce studio-grade 48kHz. Realistic ceiling for a careful blind test with your data.
- data needed: 1 min gives a decent few-shot clone; ~30-60 min of clean, well-segmented audio is the sweet spot for a near-indistinguishable fine-tune (your data is ideal). v3/v4 need good-quality training audio; v2/v2Pro tolerate average audio better.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: maybe** — This is the crucial nuance and the reason it beats StyleTTS2 on the gate. The official README ships a Mac path: `bash install.sh --device <MPS|CPU>`, and explicitly states GPU (MPS) training on Mac yields significantly lower quality so it 'temporarily uses CPUs instead' for training — i.e. CPU fine-tuning is an officially supported, working path on Apple Silicon (unlike StyleTTS2, which has none). MPS GPU *training* is discouraged/quality-degraded, not a clean win. Independent community guide (CloseX) is blunter: 'Don't think about training on Mac yet... MPS Not Supported' and asks anyone who has trained on MPS to report back — meaning MPS training is effectively unvalidated as of 2026. Practical reading: fine-tune runs on Apple Silicon via CPU (slow — hours for the GPT+SoVITS stages on 30-60 min of audio, but it completes and quality holds); MPS acceleration for training is unreliable/degraded. No CUDA-only hard blockers (no DeepSpeed/bitsandbytes/flash-attn in the training loop). Verdict: yes via CPU, no/degraded via MPS. See github.com/RVC-Boss/GPT-SoVITS README and blog.closex.org/posts/5da79853/.
- inference AS: mps — README tests Python 3.10/torch 2.5.1 and 3.11/torch 2.7.0 on Apple Silicon; MPS inference supported (CPU fallback needs model.half()->model.float()). Inference is fast and well-trodden on M-series.
- prosody/style: Reference-audio driven: the reference clip sets timbre AND emotion/prosody; supports multiple reference audios to average/blend emotion. Native controls include a speech-speed factor, top_k/top_p/temperature on the autoregressive GPT stage (prosody diversity), and text punctuation for pauses/cadence. Multilingual (zh/en/ja/ko/yue). No absolute per-word timing tokens, but emotion via reference selection is its strongest lever.
- latency: Real-time-capable inference on M-series via MPS; two-stage (AR token gen + SoVITS vocoding) adds slight overhead but fine for a local app.
- verdict: Strongest contender: top-tier speaker similarity AND the only one of the two with an officially supported Apple-Silicon training path (CPU). MPS GPU training is discouraged, and CPU training is slow, but it genuinely runs locally, free, no CUDA — and hits the indistinguishability bar with your data.
- urls: https://github.com/RVC-Boss/GPT-SoVITS, https://github.com/RVC-Boss/GPT-SoVITS/blob/main/README.md, https://blog.closex.org/posts/5da79853/, https://medium.com/axinc-ai/gpt-sovits-a-zero-shot-speech-synthesis-model-with-customizable-fine-tuning-e4c72cd75d87, https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/7051/gpt-sovits-voice-cloning-guide

### StyleTTS2 (TTS)
- license: MIT (code). Pre-trained LibriTTS/LJSpeech weights carry an ethical-use clause (must disclose synthesis / get consent) but are freely downloadable, not gated, not non-commercial. Redistributable. | free-redistributable: True
- fidelity: 8/10 — genuinely human-level naturalness and expressive prosody via style diffusion; strong similarity after fine-tuning, though zero-shot similarity is weaker than GPT-SoVITS and it can need alpha/beta tuning to lock onto exact timbre. Ceiling is high for naturalness, a notch below GPT-SoVITS for exact-timbre blind matching.
- data needed: ~1 hour (min ~1k samples) for the documented 50-epoch fine-tune; your 30-60 min sits at the low end and would work — but only on a CUDA box, which defeats the local-Mac constraint.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: no** — This is the disqualifier. There is NO Apple-Silicon training path — README/docs reference only NVIDIA (fine-tune = ~4h on 4x A100) and Windows CUDA 11.8; MPS is never mentioned. The sanctioned launch command is `accelerate launch --mixed_precision=fp16 ... train_finetune_accelerate.py` — fp16 mixed precision is a CUDA feature and is not supported on MPS, so the documented recipe won't run as-is on a Mac. The training loop is heavy and fragile even on CUDA (NaN losses unless batch size >=16, OOM tuning) and stacks a WavLM SLM adversarial objective + style diffusion + a monotonic_align C-extension + STFT/Conv1d ops that are exactly the MPS-flaky operations (see pytorch #134416 Conv1d channel limits, #86195 MPS fallback often no-ops). No community report of a successful MPS or CPU fine-tune exists as of 2026. CPU-only fine-tune is theoretically possible via PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK but would take weeks (a 4-hour 4xA100 job) and the fallback itself frequently fails on the conv ops involved. Effectively cannot fine-tune on this Mac.
- inference AS: mps — inference runs on M-series with MPS + CPU fallback (multiple pip packages, e.g. styletts2 on PyPI, work on Mac). Inference is fine; training is the problem.
- prosody/style: Strong native controls: alpha (timbre weighting), beta (prosody weighting), embedding_scale (emotionality / how much style is conditioned on text), diffusion_steps, and optional reference audio to copy style. alpha=beta=0 -> most similar/deterministic to reference; higher -> more diverse/expressive. Diffusion samples a suitable style even with no reference. No explicit rate/pause tokens, but the richest continuous style-control surface of the two.
- latency: Fast, near-real-time inference on M-series; the model is lightweight at inference relative to training.
- verdict: Disqualified on the decisive gate: excellent naturalness and the best style-control knobs, but there is no working way to fine-tune it on an Apple-Silicon Mac (CUDA-fp16 recipe, MPS-flaky ops, no community success). Great for local inference of a pre-trained/externally-trained model — not for training on this Mac.

### CosyVoice 2 (FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice, CosyVoice2-0.5B) (TTS)
- license: Apache-2.0 (code). CosyVoice2-0.5B weights also released under Apache-2.0 on ModelScope/HuggingFace. Free, redistributable, commercial-OK. No gating, no NC clause. | free-redistributable: True
- fidelity: 9/10 — SOTA zero-shot speaker similarity + very low WER; among the best open cloners in 2025-2026. Fine-tuning on a single target speaker pushes it to near-indistinguishable for that speaker, its strongest use case.
- data needed: Zero-shot needs only 3-10s reference. For fine-tune-to-indistinguishable, ~30-60 min clean target audio is a good fit (community LoRA runs use small speaker sets); more data mainly helps style coverage, not raw timbre.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: maybe (realistically no). MPS support that landed (PR #1129 / commit 029f931) is INFERENCE-only and 'limited' — it relies on PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK=1 to shove unsupported ops onto CPU, disables ONNX/TensorRT, and the install guide even recommends CPU-only PyTorch. No evidence anyone has run the TRAINING loop on MPS/CPU; training scripts target CUDA multi-GPU (torch_ddp or DeepSpeed). The 0.5B LLM + flow-matching + HiFiGAN stack could in principle CPU-train the LoRA path, but it is untested, glacially slow, and the flow/Matcha components hit MPS-unsupported ops.** — Training engines are PyTorch DDP or DeepSpeed ZeRO, launched via CUDA multi-GPU scripts in examples/libritts (DeepWiki Training guide: https://deepwiki.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/6-training). Community LoRA fine-tune done in <1hr on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, not on Mac (https://instavar.com/blog/ai-production-stack/CosyVoice_LoRA_Finetuning_Production_Guide). Apple-Silicon support is explicitly framed as inference: PR #1129 'Add limited support for MPS devices' (https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/pull/1129) and issue #134 'Support GPU accelerated inference for MacOS on apple silicon' (https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/issues/134). MPS still needs PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK=1 for missing ops (issue #228 pattern). mlx-tune, the main MLX TTS fine-tuner, does NOT support CosyVoice (https://github.com/ARahim3/mlx-tune). Net: no demonstrated MPS/CPU fine-tune exists.
- inference AS: mps (partial, with CPU fallback) — works but slower than CUDA; ONNX/TensorRT accel unavailable on Mac. CPU also viable.
- prosody/style: Strong and native: instructed generation via natural-language instructions (emotion, speaking style, dialect, accent, role) plus fine-grained inline tags — [laughter], [breath], <strong> emphasis, pace/volume; speed factor control; zero-shot cross-lingual timbre transfer. Prosody is inherited from the reference clip in zero-shot mode, and shaped further by instruction text.
- latency: Streaming/bidirectional design; ~150ms first-packet latency on GPU. On Mac MPS/CPU expect several× real-time slower; usable offline but not snappy.
- verdict: Best-in-class open cloner with an ideal Apache-2.0 license and the richest native prosody controls — but the decisive Mac-fine-tune gate is red: MPS support is inference-only and local fine-tuning on Apple Silicon is untested and CUDA-assumed. Contender ONLY if you accept doing the fine-tune on a rented/borrowed CUDA box and then running inference locally on the Mac.

### Llasa (HKUSTAudio Llasa-1B/3B/8B) + X-Codec2 (TTS (LLaMA-based, single-codebook speech LM))
- license: PROBLEM: Llasa-3B/8B weights are CC-BY-NC-4.0 (NON-COMMERCIAL, explicitly for ethics/privacy reasons) AND derive from Llama-3.2 under the Llama Community License (its own use restrictions). X-Codec2 code is on GitHub (permissive-ish) but the LM weights gate the whole system. Fine for private/local non-commercial use; NOT freely commercially redistributable. | free-redistributable: False
- fidelity: 8/10 (3B), ~8.5/10 (8B) — very good speaker similarity that scales with model size and compute; single-codebook design captures timbre+prosody. Slightly behind CosyVoice 2 on out-of-the-box similarity/WER in most 2025 comparisons, but fine-tuning narrows the gap.
- data needed: ~30-60 min is workable for LoRA speaker adaptation, but Llasa benefits from more data (its quality scales with training-time compute); 30-60 min is on the lean side for hitting indistinguishable vs CosyVoice's tighter data efficiency.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: no (maybe with heavy DIY). The reference trainer (zhenye234/LLaSA_training) is torchrun multi-GPU with DeepSpeed ZeRO-2/3 configs and no MPS path. Fine-tuning a 3B/8B Llama on MPS is memory-heavy and slow; xcodec2 (the required audio tokenizer) is CUDA-oriented with custom VQ ops and no demonstrated MPS support. mlx-tune (MLX TTS fine-tuner) does NOT list Llasa. A determined user could tokenize audio offline and LoRA-fine-tune the 1B via HF Trainer on MPS/CPU, but there is zero community evidence it works end-to-end on Mac.** — Fine-tune repo uses `torchrun --nproc_per_node=8 train_tts.py` with ds_config_zero2/zero3.json (DeepSpeed) — PyTorch-native distributed, not Mac-friendly (fetch of https://github.com/zhenye234/LLaSA_training shows DeepSpeed configs, no MPS mention). Base is meta-llama/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct with xcodec2==0.1.5 required for tokenization (https://huggingface.co/HKUSTAudio/Llasa-3B). X-Codec2 repo: https://github.com/zhenye234/X-Codec-2.0. mlx-tune explicitly supports Orpheus/OuteTTS/Spark-TTS/CSM/Qwen3-TTS but NOT Llasa (https://github.com/ARahim3/mlx-tune). No GitHub issue or blog documents a successful Apple-Silicon Llasa fine-tune.
- inference AS: cpu/mps in principle (it's a plain Llama LM + codec), but official examples are CUDA-only (model.to('cuda')); xcodec2 decode on MPS is unverified and likely needs CPU fallback. No MLX build ships.
- prosody/style: Weak/implicit. No native instruction or emotion/speed tags — it's a pure next-token speech LM; prosody, timbre and cadence all come from the reference/prompt audio and from what the fine-tune data contains. Control is via in-context reference audio, not explicit knobs. Scaling inference-time compute (sampling) can improve expressiveness.
- latency: Autoregressive token generation over a 65,536-entry codebook; 3B/8B is slow on CPU/MPS. Expect well below real-time on Mac without CUDA.
- verdict: Capable LLaMA-based cloner, but fails the two hard gates that matter most: license is non-commercial/gated, and there is no demonstrated Apple-Silicon fine-tune path (DeepSpeed/torchrun + CUDA-oriented xcodec2, and mlx-tune doesn't support it). Not a serious contender for a free, redistributable, Mac-trainable app — reserve only for private local experimentation.

### VibeVoice (1.5B / 7B) (TTS)
- license: MIT (code + weights). Microsoft pulled the official microsoft/VibeVoice repo in Sept 2025 over deepfake-misuse concerns, but the MIT grant already made it irrevocably redistributable; a community fork (vibevoice-community/VibeVoice) and HF weight mirrors preserve it. Free, commercial-OK, redistributable. | free-redistributable: True
- fidelity: 9/10 — highest expressiveness/naturalness ceiling of the two: LLM (Qwen2) backbone + diffusion acoustic head, purpose-built for long-form, multi-speaker, emotionally natural speech; strong even zero-shot, and LoRA on 30-60 min should push speaker similarity very high. Ceiling capped in practice by the fact that only the 1.5B is Mac-feasible; the 7B (best quality) needs 48GB.
- data needed: Zero-shot works from seconds; for near-indistinguishable, 30-60 min clean 24kHz audio is a reasonable LoRA target, though 1-3 h would firm up consistency. Data amount is not the bottleneck — trainability on Mac is.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: no** — The ONLY fine-tuning implementation is the unofficial voicepowered-ai/VibeVoice-finetuning repo, which is entirely CUDA/RunPod-targeted (tested on runpod/pytorch:2.8.0-cuda12.8.1), pins transformers==4.51.3, uses bf16 mixed precision + gradient checkpointing, and states 16GB VRAM min for 1.5B / 48GB for 7B. It contains ZERO references to MPS/Mac/Apple and no one reports training it on Apple Silicon. Apple-Silicon community work (sammcj/VibeVoice4macOS, rcarmo fork, microsoft/VibeVoice PR #382) is inference-only, and even that had to force SDPA over FlashAttention and switch MPS dtype to fp16 to fit memory. Training adds a diffusion-head backward pass + optimizer state; bf16 autograd on MPS is historically flaky and PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK silently drops unsupported ops to CPU, which would make a training loop crawl or error. Verdict: technically not blocked by CUDA-only libs (no deepspeed/bitsandbytes/flash-attn hard dependency for training), so it MIGHT run on a 32-64GB unified-memory Mac after porting device code — but this is completely unproven and would be a research project, not a supported path. https://github.com/voicepowered-ai/VibeVoice-finetuning https://github.com/sammcj/VibeVoice4macOS https://github.com/microsoft/VibeVoice/pull/382 https://huggingface.co/microsoft/VibeVoice-1.5B/discussions/17
- inference AS: mps — works via community forks with --device mps (fp16, SDPA attention); slower than CUDA but functional. CPU fallback exists.
- prosody/style: No explicit knobs (no rate/pause/emphasis/emotion tags). Prosody, emotion and turn-taking are controlled implicitly by the reference-voice prompt and the text/dialogue context; the model auto-infers expressive delivery. Great naturalness, low direct controllability.
- latency: Long-form optimized (up to ~90 min, 4 speakers) but slow on MPS; not real-time on Mac. Diffusion decoding adds latency.
- verdict: Highest fidelity ceiling and cleanly free/MIT, but DISQUALIFIED-until-proven on the decisive gate: no evidence anyone has fine-tuned it on Apple-Silicon MPS, and the only trainer is CUDA-targeted. Great inference on Mac, unproven training on Mac.

### Chatterbox (Resemble AI, 0.5B) (TTS)
- license: MIT (code + weights, Resemble AI). Free, commercial-OK, redistributable. Caveat: every output carries an imperceptible Perth neural watermark (does not affect perceived similarity, but is embedded by design). | free-redistributable: True
- fidelity: 8/10 — 0.5B Llama-backbone AR T3 + S3Gen flow/vocoder trained on ~0.5M h; benchmarks/blind prefs have it beating ElevenLabs on some evals. Excellent short-form speaker similarity and it has real emotion control; slightly below VibeVoice for long-form expressive range due to smaller model, but with 30-60 min LoRA it can get very close to indistinguishable on typical-length clips.
- data needed: LoRA toolkits recommend 30+ min minimum, 3-10 s segments; 30-60 min clean audio is squarely in the sweet spot for a high-similarity fine-tune, with more data improving consistency toward indistinguishable.
- **finetune on Apple Silicon: maybe** — Multiple maintained fine-tune toolkits exist (gokhaneraslan/chatterbox-finetuning [Apache-2.0], stlohrey/chatterbox-finetuning, davidbrowne17/chatterbox-streaming). They support both LoRA (is_lora=True, ~60% less VRAM, recommended <10 h data, 30+ min minimum) and full fine-tune, built on the HF Trainer, and explicitly recommend MPS-friendly settings (bf16=True over fp16, per_device_train_batch_size=1, dataloader_num_workers=0, dataloader_pin_memory=False) — the exact knobs used for MPS training. No hard dependency on deepspeed/bitsandbytes/flash-attn in the LoRA path. This is a MUCH more plausible MPS training target than VibeVoice: 0.5B params, autoregressive (no diffusion head to backprop), and inference on MPS is thoroughly proven (Jimmi42 Apple-Silicon build, resemble-ai issue #357/#85 fixes via map_location/CPU-then-MPS). HOWEVER: I found NO explicit, reproduced report of a full LoRA fine-tune completed end-to-end on an M-series MPS device — the toolkits are still primarily CUDA-tested, and known MPS gotchas remain ('Placeholder storage has not been allocated on MPS device', torch.load CUDA-deserialization errors needing patches). So: likely achievable with modest patching, but treat as 'maybe/verify' rather than turnkey. https://github.com/gokhaneraslan/chatterbox-finetuning https://github.com/stlohrey/chatterbox-finetuning https://github.com/davidbrowne17/chatterbox-streaming https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/issues/357 https://huggingface.co/Jimmi42/chatterbox-tts-apple-silicon-code
- inference AS: mps — well-established: dedicated Apple-Silicon builds run on M1-M4 with MPS GPU (load to CPU then move to device; map_location fixes for CUDA-saved checkpoints). CPU fallback works too.
- prosody/style: Best native controls of the two: an industry-first 'exaggeration' emotion knob (~0.1 calm to 1.0 dramatic), plus cfg_weight/pace control that trades pacing vs. adherence. No fine-grained SSML pause/rate tags, but real dial-able emotion intensity — a genuine advantage for matching a target's delivery.
- latency: Small AR model; near real-time on CUDA, usable (slower) on MPS. A Turbo variant improves speed further.
- verdict: The stronger practical bet for local + free + Mac-trainable: MIT, small AR model, mature MPS inference, LoRA toolkits with MPS-oriented settings, and a real emotion control knob — but the on-Mac fine-tune is 'plausible/verify', not yet demonstrated turnkey, so budget time to port CUDA-isms and validate a training run before trusting it.
